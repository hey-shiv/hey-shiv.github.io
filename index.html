    Structure-Aware Hybrid Retrieval for Cover Song Identification

    Da-TACOS

    A cover-song retrieval pipeline built on the Da-TACOS benchmark. The classical half is working: chroma/HPCP features, key invariance through the Optimal Transposition Index, cosine cross-similarity, and subsequence DTW as the alignment score that ranks candidates. Retrieval quality is measured with MAP, MRR, and Recall@{1,10,100} under WID/PID-aware splits, all unit tested against hand-worked examples.

Research direction / The through-line

Building toward machines that understand music.

Two recordings of the same song can share almost no samples, no key, no tempo, no instruments, and no voice—and a person still recognises them as the same piece in a few seconds. Whatever survives all that transformation is the part of music worth representing.

Audio Explorer taught me how sound becomes numbers. Cover song identification is where that stops being a visualisation exercise: the model has to hold onto musical identity while everything around it changes. It is a small, well-posed version of the question I actually care about—how can a system learn the structure, meaning, and feeling in music?

Engineering Philosophy

  • Build the classical baseline before the neural one, or you will never know what the network bought you.
  • Evaluation protocol is part of the result; a split that leaks makes every number meaningless.
  • Write the metric yourself and unit test it against examples worked out by hand.
  • A representation is defined by what it discards, so be explicit about what you threw away.
  • Honest failure analysis is more useful than cherry-picked results.

Failure Analysis / Lab Notes

Problems worth making visible.

  • Key invariance is an assumption A single global transposition index fails on covers that modulate partway through.
  • Clique leakage Splitting by track instead of by work puts the same composition on both sides of the wall.
  • Alignment cost is length-biased Unnormalised DTW scores quietly reward short candidates over musically better matches.
  • Noise mistaken for progress Retrieval deltas need work-level bootstrap intervals before they mean anything.
  • A control you forgot to match In the VCP study, controls drawn from the wrong window measured market regime rather than the pattern.

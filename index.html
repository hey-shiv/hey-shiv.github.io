03 — Cover song retrieval Building

Cover Song Retrieval

Can a machine recognise a song it has never heard?

01 / 05 Input / audio Illustrative

  1. Stage 01 — Signal

    One recording, as numbers.

    Nothing in the samples says which composition this is. Two recordings of the same piece may share no surface audio properties at all.

  2. Stage 02 — Representation

    Twelve pitch classes per frame.

    Chroma folds the spectrum onto twelve pitch classes. Timbre goes, on purpose: a piano and a voice singing the same harmony should look alike.

  3. Stage 03 — Key invariance

    Twelve possible keys, one chosen.

    The Optimal Transposition Index scores all twelve shifts and picks one. It also assumes the key never changes mid-song — a failure I have not solved.

  4. Stage 04 — Alignment

    Two performances, warped onto each other.

    Covers stretch and compress time, so subsequence DTW finds an elastic path between the two sequences instead of demanding they line up.

  5. Stage 05 — Retrieval

    A ranked list, and a metric that can be trusted.

    MAP, MRR and Recall@K are written from scratch and unit tested by hand — so when a learned encoder arrives, it has something real to beat.

What I learned

The evaluation protocol decides more of the result than the model does. Splitting by track instead of by work puts the same composition on both sides of the wall, and every number after that is meaningless.

04 — Audio explorer Shipped

Audio Explorer

What does a model actually see when it hears a song?

An interactive toolkit that makes the standard audio-AI front end inspectable: waveform and signal health, STFT spectrograms, 128-band Mel spectrograms, MFCCs with delta features, and beat tracking. Feature computation is kept separate from visualisation so each stage can be read on its own.

Representation Amplitude over time. Loudness, silence, clipping — the questions you ask before anything else.

What I learned

A representation is defined by what it throws away. Building each transform by hand made it obvious how much of the signal is gone by the time a model sees an MFCC — and why that is usually the point.

05 — VCP screener Negative result

VCP Screener

A screener that scans roughly 2,300 NSE equities for a volatility contraction pattern, combining technical, fundamental and ownership evidence behind a FastAPI service and a dependency-free frontend.

The screener is the smaller half. The larger half is the ten-year study that tried to falsify it: point-in-time signal replay, one shared trade simulator, real transaction costs, a portfolio-level constraint, and matched random entries as a control.

Study design

Result

No demonstrable edge.

What I learned

A control you forgot to match measures something else. Drawn from the wrong window, my random entries were reporting the market regime rather than the pattern — which is exactly how a result that looks good gets published.

06 — Failure graph

Five things I got wrong. Two are still open.

Legend Solid ring — corrected  ·  Dashed ring — still open

Clique leakage

Assumption
Splitting the dataset by track would keep train and test properly separate.
What broke
The same composition sat on both sides of the boundary, so the metric was partly measuring memorisation.
What changed
Splits are made at the work level, before anything else runs.
Status
Corrected.

Key invariance is an assumption

Assumption
One global transposition index per pair is enough to handle a change of key.
What broke
Covers that modulate partway through fall out of alignment; a single shift cannot describe them.
What changed
Nothing yet. Handling it properly means scoring more than one hypothesis per pair.
Status
Open.

Alignment cost is length-biased

Assumption
Accumulated DTW cost is a fair similarity score between two tracks.
What broke
Unnormalised cost quietly rewards short candidates over musically better matches.
What changed
Score normalisation is the current problem in the pipeline.
Status
Open.

Noise mistaken for progress

Assumption
A higher average retrieval metric means a better system.
What broke
Deltas moved around inside the noise, so the ranking of two methods was not stable.
What changed
Work-level bootstrap intervals became part of the loop rather than an afterthought.
Status
Corrected.

A control I forgot to match

Assumption
Comparing the screener’s signals against random entries is a fair control.
What broke
Controls drawn from the wrong window measured the market regime rather than the pattern.
What changed
Matched on symbol, risk and window — and the apparent edge disappeared.
Status
Corrected.

07 — Build protocol

Rules I earned the slow way.

  1. 01Build the classical baseline first, or you will never know what the network actually bought you.
  2. 02The evaluation protocol is part of the result. A split that leaks makes every number after it meaningless.
  3. 03Write the metric yourself and unit test it against a ranking worked out by hand.
  4. 04A representation is defined by what it discards, so be explicit about what you threw away.
  5. 05Try to falsify your own result before someone else has to.

Project / Coil

The stock screener I open every evening.

I swing trade NSE stocks, and 2,300 charts are too many to read by hand every evening, so Coil reads them first. Each night it scans every listed stock, narrows the market to a handful worth a look, and puts the chart, the numbers and the trade plan for each one on a single screen. The last call stays mine.

01 / 03 Pattern / contraction Schematic

  1. Stage 01 — Pattern

    Each pullback shallower than the last.

    The setup I trade most is Mark Minervini’s Volatility Contraction Pattern: inside a base, every pullback is smaller than the one before and volume dries up, until price breaks out through the pivot. Coil finds the swings in the last ~130 sessions, checks that each contraction is smaller than the one before, and that volume in the last one is below the base average.

  2. Stage 02 — Scan

    Two thousand stocks, narrowed to a handful.

    Conditions live in YAML rather than code and run in tiers: technical checks on cached prices for every stock, fundamentals only for the survivors, ownership filings only for what is left. A typical run goes 2,301 → 30 → 20 → 7, so only about thirty stocks ever need a network call.

  3. Stage 03 — Check

    Every rule replayed over ten years.

    Before I lean on a rule, I replay it over ten years of daily bars using only what was known on each date, with real costs deducted. That tells me which filters are worth weighting and which are noise, so the scan stays a filter for my judgement rather than a signal I follow blindly.

What I use it for

  • VCP screener Candidates come up as a ledger: each row draws its contractions as a small glyph and a risk rail that places the price between its stop and its pivot. A detail drawer holds the chart, financials, peers, quarterly FII / DII / promoter holdings and the stock’s NSE filings.
  • NSE Screens A query language for the whole market: Return 1M > 10 AND Volume > 1,00,000, with AND / OR / NOT, brackets, arithmetic between metrics and Indian number suffixes (Market Cap > 500Cr). It autocompletes metric names, flags typos, and treats a missing value as “could not be judged” rather than a fail. Twenty-two ready-made screens; checked against NSE Market Lens on the same session, one screen matched 192 of its 193 stocks.
  • My Scan My own EMA breakout-and-retest swing strategy, written as one bar-by-bar state machine that both the nightly scan and its backtest run. Each stock shows its stage (forming, waiting for retest, retesting, entry triggered, in trade) with the entry, stop, 2R target and quantity already worked out.
  • Sectors NSE’s four-level industry tree (16 macro sectors down to 193 basic industries) as a searchable picker that narrows any screen, plus a sector-flow view with a treemap, a rotation map and breadth figures to see where money is moving.
  • Charts Weekly above daily beside every list, stepping through results with the arrow keys. Daily, weekly, 15-minute and 1-minute bars, an EMA ribbon, and the VCP drawn on top: each contraction shaded, measured and numbered.
  • Trade management Position size from account size and percent risk, a watchlist that follows a trade from watching to closed, alerts on pivot crosses and stop breaches, a warning when one sector crowds the list, and a morning brief that sums it up.

Build

2,300 NSE stocks, every night ~18,000 lines of Python 475 passing tests 22 ready-made screens 193 basic industries

A vcp/ package holds detection, indicators, fundamentals, the screen language, sector classification and the backtest, with the trade rules in a pure domain/ layer that touches no database, network or clock. Prices, fundamentals and filings are cached in SQLite. A FastAPI layer serves it, and a dependency-free JavaScript frontend renders the views, with TradingView’s lightweight-charts vendored locally.

The frontend is hosted on Vercel and the API on Render’s free plan, kept inside its 512 MB limit. A nightly GitHub Actions job refreshes prices for every stock, precomputes the market panels, tops up fundamentals and shareholding stalest-first under a time budget, and publishes a snapshot the API reloads hourly.

Architecture

The rule I built around: what I check against history has to be literally what the app shows me tonight. Detection, trade rules and research are kept as three lanes that share one trade simulator, so a rule cannot mean one thing on the screen and another in the replay.

Design decisions

  • One trade simulator Entry, stop and exit rules once existed in three copies. They now live in one pure module, vcp/domain/execution.py, used by the app, the scans and the research, so the stop I see on screen is the stop that was tested.
  • A stop that doesn’t trail Replaying the history showed a 20-day EMA trail sold winners during normal pullbacks, so the default holds to the horizon. The choice is recorded as data in one YAML file and can be switched back on.
  • Unknown is not failing Yahoo’s fundamentals for Indian smallcaps are often missing. A stock with no P/E neither passes nor fails P/E < 15; it is shown as unjudged, so a screen never silently hides a stock I might want.
  • Checked against the source Ratios are reconciled with screener.in, promoter holding comes only from NSE’s filings (Yahoo’s figure was off by 17 points for one stock), and screen results are compared with NSE Market Lens on the same session.

What I took from it

Building a tool I trade with made me care about data quality more than any coursework did. A wrong promoter holding or a stale P/E stops being an abstract error the moment I’m about to place an order on it.

It also changed how I read the output. A scan gives me a shortlist and saves me the hours of finding and measuring setups. The decision stays with me: base quality, sector context, earnings and news.

Notes

  • Coil is a personal analysis tool, not investment advice.
  • Fundamentals for small caps fill in over a few nights; anything important is checked against the filings.
  • Intraday bars come from Yahoo on request and cover only the last 7 days (1-minute) or 60 days (15-minute).

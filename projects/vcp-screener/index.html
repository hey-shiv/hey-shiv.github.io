Project / VCP Screener

A screener that scans 2,300 stocks, and a study that says its pattern does not work.

The VCP Screener finds NSE equities building a volatility contraction pattern. It is paired with a ten-year control study that asked whether the pattern's timing beats arbitrary timing—and the answer, reported in full, is no.

  • Quantitative research
  • Backtesting
  • FastAPI
  • Negative result

Overview

A volatility contraction pattern is Mark Minervini's setup: each pullback inside a base gets shallower while volume dries up, coiling ahead of a breakout through the pivot. The screener detects it across roughly 2,300 listed NSE equities and combines three layers of evidence—technical (trend template, ATR squeeze, contraction detection), fundamental (market cap, quarterly profit and sales growth, result dates), and ownership (quarterly FII, DII, and promoter holdings parsed from NSE filings).

The screener is the smaller half of the project. The larger half is the research that tried to falsify it, and the engineering discipline needed to make that research trustworthy: point-in-time signal replay, a single shared trade simulator, real transaction costs, and a portfolio-level constraint.

Core question

Does VCP timing beat arbitrary timing in the same stocks at the same risk?

Answer

No demonstrable edge. Under a realistic capital constraint the signals do worse than random entries.

Build

2300 NSE equities scanned ~8900 lines of Python 227 tests 10 years of daily bars 2657 replayed signals

A flat vcp/ package holds detection, indicators, fundamentals, sector flow, backtesting, and the portfolio simulator. A FastAPI layer serves it, and a dependency-free vanilla JavaScript frontend renders the screener, the detail drawer, and the sector views. TradingView's lightweight-charts is vendored locally rather than pulled from a CDN, with the VCP structure drawn as an overlay: each contraction shaded, measured, and numbered, with converging lines through the swing highs and lows so the coil is actually visible.

Architecture

The organising constraint is that what gets backtested has to be literally what gets traded. Detection, simulation, and evaluation are kept as three lanes so a change to the strategy cannot silently mean one thing in the live screen and another in the research sweep.

The Control

A long-hold rule scored over a bull decade looks profitable whether or not the pattern contributed anything. So every result is compared against matched random entries: same symbol, same stop distance as a percentage of entry, same holding rule—only the date is randomised, and only inside the same window as the signals it is being compared against. That isolates exactly one variable.

Getting this wrong is easy, and I did at first. Controls drawn from the whole decade compared against real signals from one sub-period measure the difference between two market regimes, not the pattern. Every number below uses period-matched controls.

Results

Portfolio level, eight concurrent slots, real charges deducted at exit, VCP signals ranked by relative strength whenever slots ran short—an advantage the random control does not get:

Fewer trades, worse timing, twice the drawdown, and less than half the return. The per-trade numbers are closer to breakeven and noisy, but under a realistic capital constraint the pattern-selected portfolio loses on every axis. Whatever value the screener has, it is not this backtest's mechanical execution of it.

Where the Filters Fail

Slicing the signal set by sub-universe was meant to find the conditions under which the pattern works. It found the opposite: the three conditions the screen relies on most are the worst performers in the set.

  • RS ≥ 90 The single most heavily weighted filter in the config. Mean yearly edge −1.94 over matched random entries, positive in one year out of eight.
  • Tight final base (≤ 5%) The defining feature of the pattern itself. Mean yearly edge −0.64 across 2,031 signals.
  • Index above its 200 DMA Mean yearly edge −0.71, independently reproducing the regime-filter result found earlier in the study.
  • The apparent inversions RS < 70 and thin names score positive, but these are small unstable samples over fewer years—the kind of result that appears when you slice a noisy dataset eleven ways. The honest reading is that the filters carry no reliable directional information at all.

Engineering Problems Found

The architecture review that followed the research is written against the system's own weaknesses rather than its features.

  • Two trade simulators The backtest and the research sweep simulated entry, stops, and exits separately. Their logic was character-for-character identical, which is exactly the risk: the next edit makes them disagree silently, and two backtests quietly modelling different things is how a strategy result stops meaning anything. A third copy lived in the live UI's trailing-stop code.
  • The strategy was not one object Entry filters in YAML, exit rules in a research dataclass, trailing stop in the watchlist module, sizing partly in JavaScript, costs nowhere. Nothing forced them to agree—and they did not. The live app trailed on a 20-EMA after 1R while the research sweep had found trailing actively hurts.
  • Five different caches Different eviction, different invalidation, no shared contract between them.
  • An unreproducible number Several figures from the first pass did not reproduce exactly, because the script that produced them was never saved. Every follow-up now ships as a named script with its command line recorded.

Lessons Learned

This project is the reason I care as much about evaluation protocol as about models. A screener that produces confident-looking candidates is easy. A control that proves those candidates are no better than arbitrary ones is harder, and far more useful. The costs analysis made the point sharply: transaction costs tax the real signals and the random control almost identically, so they wash out of the edge metric entirely while mattering enormously to absolute returns. Anyone reading only the edge number would have missed it.

Reporting a negative result about your own system is the whole point. The document records what was tested and how, so the conclusion can be re-run and checked rather than taken on faith.

Stated Limits

  • This tests one mechanical implementation, not the idea; a discretionary trader reads base quality, sector context, earnings, and news.
  • Delisted companies are still absent, since the price frames only cover symbols listed today.
  • No position-level management: real trading scales in and out, sizes by conviction, and abandons setups that stop acting right.
  • Year buckets hold 150 to 440 trades each—enough to be suggestive, not enough to be conclusive per year.
  • The generic-breakout control is the one finding pointing the pattern's way, and it has not yet been run at the portfolio level.
